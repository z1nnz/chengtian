Three-view turnaround sheet of a mature camphor tree (Cinnamomum camphora) in a village yard, about 14 m tall. A stout trunk that forks low into three or four thick spreading limbs, grey-brown bark with fine vertical furrows. A broad, thick, wide crown wider than it is tall, but not a ball: it is built from many separate leaf masses at the ends of curving branches, with deep notches, holes of different sizes and visible limbs inside. Small glossy oval leaves, thousands of them, following the twig flow. Dense mid green with olive tones, a few warm yellow-green new leaves at the outer top, dark cool green underneath and inside.
Foliage method (most important): the crown is built from thousands of small individual solid leaves (or needle tufts) that follow curving twig-tip flows; three levels of thin branches (limb, secondary, fine twig) visibly pass in front of and behind the leaves; the crown has holes of different sizes where sky and branches show through; front and back layers of foliage give depth. Never round balls, never cloud puffs, never flat leaf cards, never a lollipop or an ellipsoid crown, never mirrored left-right branches. Not Japanese style, not bonsai.
